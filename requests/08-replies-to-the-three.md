# Request 8 — Replies to the three who answered in June 2025

**Not sent.** Drafted 15 September 2026, fifteen months after each of them wrote.

Each opens by owning the delay rather than glossing it, gives them something they did not have
before, and asks **one** thing. All three go into the existing MyHeritage threads.

**Order of importance: Halsey first.** Her answer is the closest thing to a name that exists.

---

## Reply 1 — to the closest match (297.5 cM, the Halsey line)

*She replied on 17 June 2025 naming four grandparents. Her common ancestors with Derrick are **his
own grandparents**, which makes her the most valuable correspondent in the archive.*

**Why this question and no other.** MyHeritage reads her as either Derrick's *first cousin's
daughter* or his *first cousin's granddaughter*.

- If **daughter**: her father was Derrick's first cousin — so **Mervin Halsey was a brother of
  Derrick's biological father.**
- If **granddaughter**: Mervin Halsey was himself Derrick's first cousin, and **Mervin's parent was a
  sibling of Derrick's biological father.**

**Either way the answer runs through Mervin Halsey's parents.** That is the one fact to ask for.

> Hello again — I wrote to you in June last year and you very kindly replied with your grandparents'
> names. I am sorry it has taken me this long to come back to you; the honest reason is that I only
> understood this week how useful your answer was.
>
> Since then two things have been settled about Derrick.
>
> A Y-DNA test came back: his direct paternal line is **R-S11477**, which is the North Sea branch of
> R1b — English and Dutch, not Jewish. That confirms his father of record was not his biological
> father.
>
> And of all his thousands of matches, **you are the closest.** You and he share 297 centimorgans
> across twelve segments, and MyHeritage puts your common ancestors at **his grandparents** — which
> means the connection runs through one of the two couples you named.
>
> So one question, and it is a narrow one:
>
> **Who were Mervin Halsey's parents, and where was he born?**
>
> Depending on which way the relationship falls, either Mervin was a brother of Derrick's biological
> father, or Mervin's own parent was. His parents' names would be the answer to a question this
> family has had for seventy-five years.
>
> Anything at all would help — a birth or death certificate, a place, a county, even a rough year.
> And if there is an older relative who might know, I would be glad to wait while you ask.
>
> Thank you again for answering the first time. David

## Reply 2 — to the second cousin who offered his tree (333.9 cM)

*He replied on 18 June 2025 offering everything he had, and was never answered. His tree is on
Ancestry. He also volunteered the one fact this archive cannot currently explain.*

> Hello again — you wrote to me in June last year offering all the information you had, and I never
> came back to you. That was careless of me and I am sorry. The offer, if it still stands, is now
> exactly what I need.
>
> You said something then that I did not understand at the time and still cannot account for. You had
> checked Salkow, de Witt, Luwinski and Rocha against your own matches, and said **Rocha was the only
> name that appeared.** Rocha is Derrick's *mother's* side — she was Portuguese, from Mozambique — and
> everything else says you and he are connected through his *father's* side. **Those two facts do not
> sit together,** and I would rather ask you than guess.
>
> Where things now stand: Derrick's Y-DNA is R-S11477, the North Sea branch of R1b, so his paternal
> line is English. You and he share 334 cM. You are also a first cousin of another of his matches, who
> keeps a documented tree of ten thousand people — and you both carry **Brown**. Since your paternal
> great-grandparents are Harrison, Randall, Crisp and Bray, I take it your Brown is on your
> **mother's** side.
>
> Three things, and any would help:
>
> 1. **Who were your mother's parents and grandparents?** That is where the Brown sits, and where I
>    think the connection is.
> 2. **The Rocha matches** — do you know how they arise? Names, or a place in Portugal or Mozambique?
> 3. You offered to draw out what you have, or to share it on Ancestry. **Either would be very
>    welcome.** Drawn out is fine; I do not need anything formal.
>
> Thank you for being generous the first time round, and sorry again for the delay. David

## Reply 3 — to the match at 165.9 cM (Walley Brown)

*He replied on 17 June 2025, said his sister keeps the family tree and that he would pass the message
on. Nobody followed up. He also volunteered the detail that may matter most in the whole archive.*

> Hello again — you replied to me in June last year, said your sister was the one who keeps the family
> tree, and offered to pass my message on. I never followed it up, which was my fault, and I am sorry.
>
> I am writing because of one sentence in your reply. You mentioned that your great-grandfather
> **Walley Brown was married twice — possibly at the same time** — on your mother's side.
>
> **That may be the thing I have been looking for.** Derrick was born in Johannesburg in the 1940s.
> His father of record was a Berlin Jewish refugee, and DNA has now ruled that out twice over: no
> Ashkenazi ancestry at all, and a Y-DNA result (R-S11477) that belongs to the North Sea branch of
> R1b. His biological father was an Englishman, and the evidence points at a **Brown** family — the
> same one, I think, as yours.
>
> A man with two families, one of them not properly recorded, is exactly how a branch goes missing.
>
> So:
>
> 1. **Would your sister be willing to talk to me?** I would be glad to write to her directly, or to
>    send her what I have, whichever is easier.
> 2. **What is known about Walley Brown** — his full name, roughly when he was born, the names of both
>    wives, and any children by either?
> 3. Is there any family memory of someone **going out to South Africa** in the 1930s or 1940s?
>
> I should say plainly that this may turn out to be awkward family history rather than a happy
> discovery, and I have no wish to cause trouble for anyone. If you would rather leave it, say so and
> that is the end of it.
>
> Thank you for answering the first time. David

   it is visible without anyone's help.
