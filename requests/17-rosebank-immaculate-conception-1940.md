# Request 17 — Immaculate Conception, Rosebank: the marriage register of 7 September 1940

**Status: SENT 15 September 2026.** Gmail thread `1a0a3b72c965ecf1`. MX re-verified on the day immediately before sending.

## Why this is the best letter on the list

Every civil marriage register this archive has read — **1940, 1948, 1951, 1952, 1956** — has the same
eleven columns, and **not one of them has a column for a parent**. That is the wall the whole
paternity question has been standing at for a week.

**Catholic sacramental registers do name parents.** And the 1940 civil register hands over the fact
that opens the door: the Denomination line is filled in, in the celebrant's own hand,
**"Catholic Priest"**, signed **W. P. Whelan** — and the marriage was **by banns**, not on a licence,
so the intention was read in church on three Sundays beforehand and generates a second entry besides.

It is a **church** record, which puts it entirely outside the Home Affairs eligibility problem that
stopped request 15. Nobody has to be an immediate family member to ask a parish a question.

## Identifying the parish

The civil register says the marriage was *solemnised at* **Rosebank**, district Johannesburg. The
Catholic parish of Rosebank is the **Church of the Immaculate Conception, 16 Keyes Avenue, Rosebank
2196**, **established 1936** — four years before the wedding, which fits. Parish office
`Info@rosebankcatholicchurch.co.za` (MX verified, Microsoft 365), 011 788 5226/7, open Monday to
Friday 09h00–15h00, **closed weekends** — so do not expect a same-day answer.

**Not asserted, and the letter says so:** the archive has not proved this is the right parish, only
that it is the Catholic parish in Rosebank and that it existed in 1940. The letter asks them to
redirect it if it is not theirs. If the register has been deposited centrally the fallback is the
**Archdiocese of Johannesburg** (`catholicjhb.org.za`, MX verified) — **confirm the chancery address
on the day; do not guess it.**

Dear Parish Office,

I am writing from Australia with a family-history enquiry about a **marriage celebrated in your
parish in 1940**, and I would be grateful for whatever you are able to tell me — including, if it is
the answer, that the records no longer exist.

## The marriage

**7 September 1940**, at **Rosebank**, Johannesburg:

- **Leonard Aubrey Robert Wear** — 23, born in England, bachelor, his occupation given as *Army* and
  his address as *Roberts Heights, Pretoria*
- **Phyllis Agnes Robertson** — 25, born in Natal, spinster, a shorthand typist, of 23 Derby Road,
  Judith Paarl, Johannesburg

The celebrant signed himself **W. P. Whelan** and wrote **"Catholic Priest"** on the denomination
line. They were married **by banns**, not by licence. The witnesses were **Mary Una Robertson** and
**James Rogers**.

That comes from the state's *Duplicate Original Marriage Register*, no. 13 of 1940, which I have read
in facsimile. **I believe yours is the Rosebank parish in question** — the Church of the Immaculate
Conception, established in 1936 — but I am working from the single word *Rosebank* on a government
form, so if this marriage belongs to another parish, or to a chaplaincy attached to the military
camp at Roberts Heights, I would be very glad to be redirected.

## What I am asking

1. **Does the parish marriage register for 1940 survive**, and does it contain this entry?
2. If it does, **does the entry name the parents of the bride and groom?** This is the whole reason
   for my letter, and I explain why below.
3. Is there a separate **record of the banns** being read?
4. Was **Fr W. P. Whelan** a priest of your parish at that time? Even confirming the name would help
   me know I am in the right place.
5. If the older registers have been **deposited with the Archdiocese of Johannesburg** rather than
   kept at the parish, could you tell me so, and I will write there instead.

## Why the parents' names matter so much

South African **civil** marriage registers of this period have no columns for parents. I have now
read five of them at full resolution — 1940, 1948, 1951, 1952 and 1956 — and not one names anybody's
father or mother. The state simply did not ask.

**Catholic registers do ask.** That single difference is why I am writing to a parish rather than to
a government office. The one fact I have been unable to document anywhere is **who this man's
parents were**, and a sacramental register of 1940 would ordinarily record them.
