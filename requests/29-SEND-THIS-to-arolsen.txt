Dear Arolsen Archives,

I am researching the Leibholz family of Berlin on behalf of my father-in-law, whose
mother was Auguste Leibholz, born Luwinski. I have the consent declaration in place and
have been able to read a good deal in the online archive. I am writing about one file
that the online archive tells me exists but does not display.

THE FILE

    T/D 52.971 — Tracing and documentation case for LEIBHOLZ, OSCAR, born 13.05.1887
    Reference code 06030302.0.052.971
    14 documents, creation date 1949 to 2002

The record page says: "Under certain circumstances, parts of the file are not displayed
for data protection reasons. In such cases, please contact the Arolsen Archives." So I am
doing that, rather than assuming.

WHY THIS FILE AND NOT ANOTHER

Oskar Leibholz, born at Schubin on 13 May 1887, was deported from Berlin to Theresienstadt
in March 1943 and murdered at Auschwitz on 16 October 1944. His wife Gertrud, born Leibke,
died on 27 February 1943. So the person who opened a tracing enquiry about him in 1949
was neither of them, and had survived.

That is what I am asking for. Not the outcome — I already know what became of him — but
THE NAME OF THE PERSON WHO ASKED, and what they said their relationship to him was.

I should be plain about why it matters so much for this particular family. Oskar was the
brother of JACOB LEIBHOLZ, born Schubin 8 August 1884, who married Auguste Luwinski in
Berlin-Schöneberg in January 1924 and was deported from Berlin on 26 February 1943.
Jacob is my father-in-law's grandfather. I have found survivors attached to other Leibholz
households in Berlin, but every one of them has turned out to belong to a different
Leibholz family — one born at Stolp in Pomerania, another at Hammerstein in West Prussia.
I have not yet found a single person who survived the war and was connected to the Schubin
family. A named enquirer on T/D 52.971 would be the first.

WHAT I AM ASKING FOR

1. Whatever of T/D 52.971 you are able to release, and in particular the identity of the
   enquirer and their stated relationship to Oskar Leibholz.

2. If part of the file must stay closed, I would be glad of whatever can be said about it
   — even "the enquirer was a sibling" or "the enquirer wrote from abroad" would be
   more than I have.

3. The file's creation date runs to 2002, more than fifty years after it was opened. If
   there was a later enquiry as well as the 1949 one, I would very much like to know that
   two different people asked, even if neither can be named.

I understand entirely if the answer is that living or recently living people are involved
and the file stays shut. If that is the answer I will not press it.

TWO THINGS THAT MAY SAVE YOU TIME

I am not a descendant of Oskar and make no claim to be. My father-in-law descends from his
brother Jacob.

And the surname is spelt several ways across your own holdings — LEIBHOLZ, LEIBHOLTZ, and
in the family's own Berlin registration card the forename appears as JACQUES where every
other record has JACOB. Oskar appears in your index both as OSKAR and as ISRAEL OSKAR,
the second being the name the 1938 decree forced on him.

I am happy to complete any form, pay any fee, supply identification, and wait.

With thanks for an archive that has already told me a great deal,
