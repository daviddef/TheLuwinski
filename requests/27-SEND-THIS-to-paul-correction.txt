Paul,

Two corrections to my last message, a find that is yours as much as mine, and then three smaller things.

THE CORRECTIONS FIRST, BECAUSE I WOULD RATHER YOU HEARD THEM FROM ME.

1. I wrote: "If Martha Giddings died in 1863 she cannot have known her son at all."

That is wrong, and I should not have written it. I had her death year and his birth year and I did the arithmetic without looking at the quarters.

She died in the THIRD quarter of 1863, registered at Hitchin, volume 3A page 188. Your George William was baptised on 4 January 1863. So she had him for something under a year, not none of it.

It also changes how the 1871 census reads. RG10/1365, folio 27, Norton End:

    James Giddins            head
    Elizabeth Giddins        wife
    GEORGE CASTLE, 30, agricultural labourer   SON-IN-LAW
    GEORGE CASTLE, 8                           GRANDSON

The father is IN the house. He is not a man who gave his son away; he is a widower who went to his dead wife's parents and stayed, and was still there eight years later. The two relationship words, son-in-law and grandson, are the census's own and not a tree's, and they only work through Martha. That is now the proof of your line above George William, and it came out of your tree. One caution on the same census: it gives the boy's birthplace as BALDOCK, not Willian, and his birth year as 1863, not 1862. I do not know which is right and I would rather tell you than change it behind your back.

2. I gave George William Castle's years as 1862 to 1916. The 1916 was another tree's, and I copied it without setting it against the death index. The index has one George Castle aged 54 who died between March 1916 and December 1917: George W Castle, registered at Hitchin in the September quarter of 1917, volume 3A page 607. Hitchin has no George Castle death in 1916 at all. So 1917, as an index match by name, age and district; I have not read the certificate. If your tree has a source for 1916 I would like to see it.

THE FIND, WHICH IS YOUR CAROLINE'S PARENTS.

Caroline's years in my last message, 1862 to 1957, were right, and I now have a better reason than a tree. She was baptised at Weston on 16 April 1862 as Caroline Wallis, daughter of Joseph, a labourer, and Sarah. In the 1871 census she is nine, a straw plaiter, in Maiden Street, Weston, in Joseph and Sarah's household with eight brothers and sisters (the index spells the surname Wallace, which is why I had not found her). In the 1881 census she is 19 and single, a straw plaiter, in the same house, with Joseph and Sarah and the surname spelt Wallis. Eighteen months later she married your George William at Hitchin.

So her parents are, very probably, JOSEPH AND SARAH WALLIS of Weston. I say probably because no certificate names her father. The match is the name, the parish, the age and the straw plaiting, and the 1939 Register and the 1911 census put her a year younger than she was, which is common. Her marriage certificate would settle it. Her first child was registered as Sarah Ann Wallis in 1882, which is her mother's name.

NOW THREE SMALLER THINGS.

1. CAROLINE DID NOT STAY A WALLIS, AND THAT IS WHY SHE CANNOT BE FOUND AFTER 1920.

Your Caroline Wallis is your great-grandmother, and every tree I can see loses her after George William died in 1917. Their own marriage is in the index, Hitchin, December quarter 1882, volume 3A page 567, with both names on the one page.

She remarried. Third quarter of 1920, registered at Hitchin, volume 3A page 2077: CAROLINE CASTLE married JAMES JAMES. Three years a widow, and then a second husband whose surname was also his forename.

FROM 1920 SHE WAS CAROLINE JAMES. That is the whole reason she disappears: anyone searching the death indexes for a Caroline Castle or a Caroline Wallis in the 1950s is looking for a name she had not used for thirty-seven years. She died in 1957 at Hitchin as CAROLINE JAMES.

2. THE 1939 REGISTER CARRIES HER DAY AND MONTH.

RG101/1598B/017/25 has her at 38 NORTON ROAD, BALDOCK, widowed a second time, aged 76, and it gives her full date of birth, 28 February, because she gave it to a registrar herself. The year it prints is 1863, which is a year out; the baptism says 1862, and a birth in the first quarter of 1862 fits 28 February.

3. AND THE SURNAME IS WALLIS, NOT WALLS, THOUGH A CENSUS INDEX SPELLS IT WALLACE.

I had this the other way round for a while. The GRO birth index can be searched on the mother's maiden name: six Castle children come back under WALLIS and none at all under Walls. Six to nothing is as settled as that kind of thing gets.

SOMETHING SMALLER, WHICH IS NOT YOUR LINE BUT MIGHT INTEREST YOU ANYWAY.

I said last time that the Wears emigrated in 1939 in two parties, and left it there as though the house simply emptied.

The 1939 Register was taken on 29 September, seven weeks after Martha Maude's ship sailed. Henry Robert and Martha Maude had raised their six at 72 PIX ROAD, Letchworth. At 63 PIX ROAD, a few doors along the same street, the register finds:

    EMMA HULL, born 7 February 1859, widowed, "Incapacitated - Blind"
    LOUISA SHENTON, born 29 November 1882, married
    WILLIAM SHENTON, born December 1876, incapacitated

Emma Hull is Emma Hawkins, Henry Robert's mother, widowed at thirty-one in 1890, remarried to John Hull in the second quarter of 1894 at St Albans, and widowed a second time. She was eighty and blind, living on her son's street with the daughter who had been eight when her father died.

The record says who was where. It does not say what anybody thought about it and I am not going to guess. But it stops being true that the family simply left.

I will send images of any of it: the 1871 Norton End page, the 1939 entries, the GRO index screens. Say the word and they come.

And thank you for the tree. Most of what is above came out of chasing names that were in it.
