A correction to my last message, and then three things that are yours rather than mine.

THE CORRECTION FIRST, BECAUSE I WOULD RATHER YOU HEARD IT FROM ME.

I wrote: "If Martha Giddings died in 1863 she cannot have known her son at all."

That is wrong, and I should not have written it. I had her death year and his birth year and I did the arithmetic without looking at the quarters.

She died in the THIRD quarter of 1863, registered at Hitchin, volume 3A page 188. Your George William was baptised on 4 January 1863. So she had him for something under a year — not none of it. Which is a small thing to be wrong about and a large thing for a woman to have had.

It also changes the shape of what you and I both noticed. Your George William is with his grandparents in 1871 and 1881, and I read that as a motherless child handed over at birth. The 1871 census reads differently once the quarters are right. RG10/1365, folio 27, Norton End:

    James Giddins            head
    Elizabeth Giddins        wife
    GEORGE CASTLE, 30, agricultural labourer   SON-IN-LAW
    GEORGE CASTLE, 8                           GRANDSON

The father is IN the house. He is not a man who gave his son away; he is a widower of twenty-seven who went to his dead wife's parents and stayed, and was still there eight years later. And the two relationship words — son-in-law, grandson — are the census's own, not a tree's. They only work through Martha. That is now the proof of your line above George William, and it came out of your tree, so thank you.

One caution attached to the same census, since I am correcting myself anyway: it gives the boy's birthplace as BALDOCK, not Willian, and his birth year as 1863, not 1862. I do not know which is right. I am telling you because your tree says Willian and I do not want to have quietly changed it behind your back.

NOW THE THREE THINGS, AND THE FIRST IS THE ONE I WOULD OPEN.

1. CAROLINE DID NOT STAY A WALLIS, AND THAT IS WHY SHE CANNOT BE FOUND.

Your Caroline Wallis, 1862-1957, is your great-grandmother, and every tree I can see loses her after George William died in 1916.

She remarried. Third quarter of 1920, registered at Hitchin, volume 3A page 2077: CAROLINE CASTLE married JAMES JAMES. Four years a widow, and then a second husband whose surname was also his forename.

FROM 1920 SHE WAS CAROLINE JAMES. That is the whole reason she disappears — anyone searching the death indexes for a Caroline Castle or a Caroline Wallis in the 1950s is looking for a name she had not used for thirty-seven years. She died in 1957 at Hitchin as CAROLINE JAMES.

2. HER DATE OF BIRTH IS 28 FEBRUARY 1863, NOT 1862.

The 1939 Register, RG101/1598B/017/25, has her at 38 NORTON ROAD, BALDOCK, widowed a second time, aged 76, and it carries her full date of birth because she gave it to a registrar herself. Every secondary source I can find says 1862. The register says 28 February 1863.

3. AND THE SURNAME IS WALLIS, NOT WALLS.

I had this the other way round for a while. The GRO birth index can be searched on the mother's maiden name: six Castle children come back under WALLIS and none at all under Walls. Six to nothing is as settled as that kind of thing gets.

SOMETHING SMALLER, WHICH IS NOT YOUR LINE BUT MIGHT INTEREST YOU ANYWAY.

I said last time that the Wears emigrated in 1939 in two parties, and left it there as though the house simply emptied.

The 1939 Register was taken on 29 September, seven weeks after Martha Maude's ship sailed. Henry Robert and Martha Maude had raised their six at 72 PIX ROAD, Letchworth. At 63 PIX ROAD, a few doors along the same street, the register finds:

    EMMA HULL, born 7 February 1859, widowed, "Incapacitated - Blind"
    LOUISA SHENTON, born 29 November 1882, married
    WILLIAM SHENTON, born December 1876, incapacitated

Emma Hull is Emma Hawkins — Henry Robert's mother, widowed at thirty-one in 1890, remarried to John Hull, and widowed a second time. She was eighty years old and blind, living on her son's street with the daughter who had been eight when her father died.

The record says who was where. It does not say what anybody thought about it and I am not going to guess. But it stops being true that the family simply left.

I will send images of any of it — the 1871 Norton End page, the 1939 entries, the GRO index screens. Say the word and they come.

And thank you for the tree. Three of the four things above came out of chasing names that were in it.
