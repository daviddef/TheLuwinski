NOTE TO DAVID BEFORE SENDING — this is an ACCESS EXAMINATION request, not an order for copies.
It is FREE. Under the Archives Act the National Archives of Australia must examine a record on
request and decide whether it is open; what it costs is time, not money. Four of the five items
below are already OPEN and need no examination at all — they are listed so the reference staff
can see the whole family in one request and tell you, in one reply, what can be copied and what
each copy would cost. NOTHING HERE COMMITS YOU TO BUYING ANYTHING.

Send it through the "Ask us about the collection" form at
naa.gov.au/help-your-research/ask-us-about-collection, or by email to ref@naa.gov.au.
Everything below is quoted from RecordSearch's own catalogue, so the staff can find each item
from the citation alone.

Subject: Access examination request — five items, Luwinski and Leibholz, 1939 to 1965

Dear National Archives of Australia,

I am researching two German families who came to Australia, and RecordSearch has led me to five
items. Three are not yet examined. I would like to request access examination of those, and to
ask what a copy of each of the five would involve.

1. THE FAMILY WHO ARRIVED IN 1965

    NAA: A2559, 1965/86/85-88 — item ID 5435314 — 9 folios — Canberra
    "LUWINSKI Manfred born 14 November 1933 ... arrived per ship FLAVIA ex Bremerhaven/Germany
    17 September 1965"
    Access status: NOT YET EXAMINED — examination requested

    Manfred Luwinski was born at Berlin on 14 November 1933 and died at Brisbane on
    20 September 2012. He is my wife's family. I hold the European half of this same document:
    the International Committee for European Migration nominal roll for this sailing survives in
    the Arolsen Archives in Germany, DocID 81709354, where the family appears at lines 85 to 88
    of roll BGO 65/135 — the same four line numbers as your control symbol. Your nine folios are
    the Australian side of a record I can currently only see one half of.

2. THE FAMILY WHO ARRIVED IN 1939

    NAA: A714, 18/8913 — item ID 31530861 — ACCESS STATUS OPEN
    "SURNAME - LEIBHOLZ; GIVEN NAMES - Joachim Peter; DATE OF NATURALISATION - 27 JUL 1944;
    PREVIOUS NATIONALITY - STATELESS; PLACE OF BIRTH - Berlin, GERMANY; YEAR OF BIRTH - 1903;
    PLACE OF RESIDENCE - South Yarra, VIC; ADDITIONAL INFORMATION - [W] LEIBHOLZ, Tilde"

    NAA: A435, 1944/4/1672 — item ID 5276024 — Canberra
    "LEIBHOLZ Joachim Peter - born 20 August 1903 - German"
    Access status: NOT YET EXAMINED — examination requested

    NAA: A12508, 21/2464 — item ID 7120530
    "LEIBHOLZ Joachim Peter born 20 August 1903; Hilde aged 26; nationality German; travelled per
    ANCHISES arriving in Melbourne on 12 June 1939"

    NAA: A1478, EPCP63 — item ID 4968389 — Canberra
    "German property taken over by the Polish Government - Leibholz J P Mr", 1947
    Access status: NOT YET EXAMINED — examination requested

    Joachim Peter Leibholz reached Melbourne ten weeks before the war and was recorded in 1944 as
    having been STATELESS. My wife's Leibholz family came from Schubin in the Prussian province
    of Posen, which is now Szubin in Poland, before moving to Berlin. Item A1478 EPCP63 concerns
    property inside the post-war Polish border belonging to a Berlin-born Leibholz, and it is the
    first document I have found anywhere that connects those two places through this surname.
    I do not yet know whether he is a relative. These files would tell me.

3. TWO SMALL QUESTIONS, IF THEY ARE EASY TO ANSWER

    (a) Item MP56/10, LEIBHOLZ T, at your Melbourne office, is catalogued as "LEIBHOLZ Tilde
        [AND CHILD]". Does the file name the child?

    (b) Is there a migrant selection document for Manfred Luwinski in series A2478, A12111 or a
        related series? A keyword search of RecordSearch for LUWINSKI, and for the variant
        spellings LUWINSKY, LUWENSKI, LOWINSKI, LOWINSKY, JUWINSKI and LUWWINSKY, returns only
        item A2559, 1965/86/85-88 — but I understand that not every series is item-listed, and a
        1965 assisted migrant would ordinarily generate one.

I am content to be told that a record is closed, or that a copy would be expensive; a clear answer
either way is useful to me. If any of these items has already been digitised since I searched,
please just point me at it.
