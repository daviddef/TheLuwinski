`sandfdoc@mweb.co.za` is still the address quoted by almost every guide to South African military

*Schweikert Building, 20 Visagie Street* is stale, and the current address is **42 Saturnus Road,

Fridays. **Send to `archive@dod.mil.za` only** — MX verified `mail1.dod.mil.za`.

**3. He was almost certainly not an officer, and the letter should not ask as if he were.**
Officers' commissions, promotions and appointments were printed in the *Government Gazette* by full
name. Gazettes.Africa is full-text searchable and the control works — *"Robert Mitford Benn"* and
*"Aubrey Wilfred Fawkes Smith"*, both officers in wartime gazettes, return exactly one document
each. **Leonard returns nothing under six spellings**: *Leonard Aubrey Robert Wear*, *Aubrey Robert
Wear*, *Wear, Leonard Aubrey*, *L. A. R. Wear*, *L.A.R. Wear*, *Leonard A. R. Wear*. So he was
other ranks. **Caveat, and it is a real one:** the OCR on these gazettes is poor — the same pages
render *Johannesburg* as *Johanoesburg* — and a surname as short as *Wear* is easily mangled. The
honest statement is *not found*, not *not there*.

## The man

**WEAR, Leonard Aubrey Robert.**

- Born **1917 in England** — birth registered at Hitchin, Hertfordshire, in the first quarter of 1917
- On **7 September 1940**, aged **23**, he married at Rosebank, Johannesburg
- On that marriage register his **occupation is entered as "Army"**
- and his **residence as "Roberts Heights, Pretoria"**

The source is the *Duplicate Original Marriage Register*, Johannesburg district, **no. 13 of 1940**;
his bride was **Phyllis Agnes Robertson**, 25, a shorthand typist of 23 Derby Road, Judith Paarl. He
gave his personal status as bachelor and his country of birth as England. He was alive in October
1951, when he married again in Johannesburg and gave his occupation as **engineer**.

## What I am asking

1. **Does a personnel or attestation record survive in his name?** I should be grateful if the
   spellings **WEAR, WEIR and WARE** could all be checked, and initials **L.A.R.**
2. **If an attestation form survives, does it name his parents or his next of kin?** This is the
   heart of the enquiry, and I will explain why below.
3. **Is he in the medal rolls** — the Africa Service Medal, the 1939–45 Star, or any other award? A
   medal roll entry would give me the **force number** I do not have, and with a number the rest
   becomes ordinary.
4. **If a search by name alone is not possible**, is there a nominal index I could be pointed to, or
   would you advise starting from the medal rolls to establish a number first?
5. Is there anything particular about **Roberts Heights in September 1940** that would narrow the
   search — units headquartered there whose records are separately listed?

## What I have already eliminated, so that you are not asked to repeat it

- He is **not a casualty**: he was alive in 1951 and for many years after.
- He appears to be **other ranks, not an officer**. I have searched the full text of the *Government
  Gazette* for his name in six spellings across the war years and found nothing, while test searches
  for named officers in the same volumes return their entries correctly. I would not put weight on
  this — the OCR of those pages is rough — but it is why I am not asking you to look for a commission.
- I have **no force number**, and I know that is the ordinary key to your holdings. What I have
  instead is an exact date, an exact address, a full name, a birth year and a wife's name.

## Two honest disclosures

**I am not a descendant of this man and I make no claim to be.** I am a family historian working on a
documented family history, and he is a person that research has led me to. If your access rules
require a relationship, I would rather be told that now than put you to any trouble.

**And I should say plainly what I am hoping for**, because it explains why question 2 matters more to
me than the rest. South African civil marriage registers of this period have no columns for
parents — I have read three of them at full resolution and none of them names anybody's father. An
attestation form does. The one thing I cannot presently document is **who this man's parents were**.
If his attestation names them, it settles a question that nothing else I have found can.
