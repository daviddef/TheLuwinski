The marriage record settled a question the family had wondered about for years, and named a
generation nobody knew. I am sincerely grateful for it.

THE QUESTION THAT REMAINS

In my previous letter I asked this conditionally — "if the entry is not there after all". The entry
was there, so the condition never arose. I therefore ask it now directly.

    Does the State Archive in Bydgoszcz hold the RECORDS OF THE JEWISH RELIGIOUS COMMUNITY
    OF SZUBIN — registers of births, marriages or deaths from before 1874?

    And if not — do you know where such registers might now be, or whether they were destroyed?

WHY THIS PARTICULARLY

I am looking for the birth record of FRIEDERICA SCHMUL. The 1874 marriage record states that she
was born at Szubin and was then about twenty years old, which points to about 1852.

I understand that Prussian civil registration began on 1 October 1874 — indeed the records show it,
since the Szubin marriage register for 1874 contains only nine entries. A birth of about 1852
therefore cannot be in the civil registers. If any record survives, it can only be in the Jewish
community registers.

I HAVE CHECKED THE PORTAL, SO AS NOT TO WASTE YOUR TIME

I could find no Jewish religious community fonds for Szubin on genealogiawarchiwach.pl. The only
religious fonds for the town is the EVANGELICAL-UNIONIST COMMUNITY OF SZUBIN (286 units). So I am
asking about holdings not published online, not about what is already on the portal.

A SECONDARY QUESTION, DEFINITELY LESS IMPORTANT

If it can be answered without much work — I do not know the date or place of death of SALLY
LEIBHOLZ (b. 1847). He was still alive in August 1884, when he registered his son's birth at
Szubin. I am not asking you to search the death registers, since I know they have no indexes; I ask
only whether any finding aid exists that would establish the date. If not, please ignore this.

AND ONE COURTEOUS REQUEST, IF THE RECORD IS TO HAND

In marriage record no. 9/1874 the printed form for the bride reads "Tochter der…", not "Tochter
des…" as it does for the groom. I cannot read the word that follows "der". It decides whether this
is "Tochter der unverehelichten…" (daughter of an unmarried woman) or "Tochter der Eheleute…"
(daughter of the married couple). If anyone reading this happens to have that scan in front of them
and would glance at it, I should be enormously grateful. I understand this is not an archive's
obligation.

I am happy to complete any form, pay any fee, supply identification, and wait. I am not a direct
descendant: my father-in-law is Jacob Leibholz's grandson.
