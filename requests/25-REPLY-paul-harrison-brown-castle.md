## Why the refusal matters more than the answer

**This is the first time anyone has asked this archive to identify one living DNA match to another.**
It will not be the last, and the answer should be the same every time: *no, but I will pass on your
details and let them decide.* **The cost of getting this wrong once is that no match ever trusts the
archive again**, and the whole Castle reconstruction exists because strangers answered letters.

**It also costs him nothing.** MyHeritage already shows him his own matches and their predicted
relationships. If she is his first cousin, she is on his own list under his own login. **What he is
actually asking for is a name to put to a row he can already see** — and that is hers to give.

## The genealogy, which is worth getting exactly right

His guess is reasonable and wrong in a specific way. **He assumed that because his match list says
BROWN, the shared ancestors must be Browns.** They are not.

- Penelope Browne, 292.7 cM, descends from **AGNES KATE CASTLE and WALTER GEORGE BROWN**.
- Derrick descends from **MARTHA MAUD CASTLE**, Agnes Kate's sister.
- **Agnes Kate and Martha Maud were both daughters of GEORGE CASTLE and CAROLINE WALLS of Baldock.**

**So Brown enters the picture only as a husband.** It is the surname carried *down* from the shared
couple on one branch, which is exactly why it dominates one match list and appears nowhere in the
other. His own 18 June 2025 message already said his paternal great-grandparents are
Harrison/Randall and Crisp/Bray — **so his Brown line is maternal, which fits.**

**333.9 cM across 16 segments** is consistent with second cousins, and a second cousinship through
George Castle and Caroline Walls is the reading. *The letter does not over-claim the exact number of
generations*, because MyHeritage's relationship estimate is a range and this archive has already been
burned once by treating a predicted relationship as a measured one.

## One thing this reply must NOT do

**It must not tell him about Derrick's paternity.** That is a different conversation, it belongs in
its own letter, and it is not owed to a man who asked about Brown great-grandparents. The Gillian Nel
letter carries it because she keeps Leonard's tree and could not reasonably be told the rest without
it. **Paul has no such need to know, and volunteering it would be gossip dressed as candour.**

**On the great-grandparents: not Brown, I think, and the reason may be useful to you.**

Your instinct that we share great-grandparents looks right. But I do not think the shared couple is
a Brown couple at all. **I think it is GEORGE CASTLE and CAROLINE WALLS of Baldock, Hertfordshire,
who married in 1883.**

Here is why. Another of my matches descends from **Agnes Kate Castle, who married Walter George
Brown**. Derrick descends from **Martha Maud Castle**. **Agnes Kate and Martha Maud were sisters** —
both daughters of George Castle, a farm labourer and later a labourer at the Baldock brewery, and
Caroline, born at Weston four miles away.

So **Brown comes into it as a husband, not as the common ancestor.** That is exactly why the surname
is all over one side of this and nowhere on the other. It also squares with what you told me last
June — that your paternal great-grandparents are Harrison/Randall and Crisp/Bray — which puts your
Brown line on your mother's side, where a Castle daughter's married name would sit.

If that is right, **Alfred and Frederick Brown are probably descendants of the couple rather than the
couple itself**, and the name to look for behind them is **Castle, at Baldock and Norton in
Hertfordshire.** The 1901 and 1911 censuses put the family at Norton End and then at St Mary's
Cottages by the church yard in Baldock. I will happily send you the images.
