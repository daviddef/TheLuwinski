# Request 7 — The two letters to living DNA matches

**Not sent. These need your decision, not mine.**

Everything else this archive has written has gone to an institution. These go to two private people
who did not ask to be contacted, about a finding that touches their family as well as this one. That
is a different kind of act, and the drafts are here for you to change, approve or refuse.

## The principles I have written to

1. **Do not open with non-paternity.** A stranger's first message should not be *"your family may
   contain my relative's unacknowledged father."* Both letters ask a narrow, answerable question and
   let the rest follow if they want it to.
2. **Ask about the dead, not the living.** Every name mentioned in both letters belongs to someone who
   has died.
3. **No surprises later.** Each says plainly that this is about a DNA connection that the paper trail
   does not explain. Nobody should feel they were drawn in under false pretences.
4. **Name Derrick, and no one else.** Consistent with the archive's own rule.
5. **Say who is asking and why, in the first two lines.** People answer honest strangers and ignore
   mysterious ones.

## Letter A — to the closest match

*297.5 cM, twelve segments. Her common ancestors with Derrick are his own grandparents. Born 1995, so
two generations below him; her pedigree is Halsey, Halsey, Parr, Parr, Fenton. Her match card records
the shared ancestral place as **South Africa**.*

> **Subject: A DNA match with a South African connection — Luwinski**
>
> Hello,
>
> I hope you don't mind a message out of the blue. I manage a DNA kit for a family member,
> **Lynn Derrick Luwinski**, born in Johannesburg in the 1940s, and you are one of his closest
> matches — you share about 297 centimorgans across twelve segments, which is a real and fairly close
> relationship.
>
> What makes me write is that MyHeritage records the ancestral place you have in common as
> **South Africa**, and Derrick's family were in Johannesburg from the 1930s.
>
> I'm not trying to sell anything or to get at anybody's private information. I'm putting together a
> documented family history, and I've run into an honest difficulty: **the DNA says you and he are
> related, and the paperwork doesn't explain how.** His father's side is German and Jewish, from
> Berlin, and that isn't where the match is coming from.
>
> If you happen to know the answer to any of these, it would help enormously:
>
> - Did any of your family go out to **South Africa** — Johannesburg in particular — and if so, who?
> - Does the surname **Halsey** run in your direct line, or does it come in by marriage?
> - Do the names **Brown**, **Castle** or **Webb** mean anything in your family?
>
> If your parents or grandparents would know better than you, I'd be glad to be pointed their way, or
> to wait. And if you'd rather not be involved at all, just say so and I won't write again.
>
> Thank you either way.

## Letter B — to the keeper of the 10,715-person tree

*292.7 cM, twelve segments, second cousin at 68% — common ancestors at great-grandparent level. In
Australia. Four of the other British matches are her own first cousins and their children.*

> **Subject: 2nd cousin DNA match — a Johannesburg branch I can't place**
>
> Hello,
>
> You and a family member of mine, **Lynn Derrick Luwinski**, are estimated second cousins — about
> 292 centimorgans across twelve segments. You clearly know what you're doing with a tree of ten
> thousand people, so I'll be direct rather than vague.
>
> Derrick was born in **Johannesburg** in the 1940s. His mother was Portuguese, from Mozambique. His
> father of record was a Berlin Jewish refugee — and the DNA says plainly that this cannot be his
> biological father: no Ashkenazi ancestry at all where a quarter would be required. **His paternal
> line is English.** That is the honest position, and it is why I am writing to you rather than
> guessing.
>
> If you and he are second cousins, then his biological father was a **first cousin of one of your
> parents** — which from your tree would put him among the descendants of
> **Alfred John Brown and Sarah Maria Brister**, **George William Castle and Caroline Wallis**, or
> **William Frederick Webb and Louisa Hayden**.
>
> Three questions, and any one of them would help:
>
> 1. Did a cousin of your father's or your mother's **go out to South Africa** — Johannesburg, around
>    the 1930s or 1940s?
> 2. Does the surname **Halsey** appear anywhere in the family, by marriage or otherwise? Derrick's
>    single closest match carries it, and her shared place with him is South Africa.
> 3. Your tree records your mother's father as **Unknown**. Is anything known about him at all —
>    even a first name or a county? I ask because a gap in exactly that generation is where this
>    connection would sit, and I'd rather ask than speculate.
>
> I realise this may be unwelcome news about someone's grandfather, and I've no wish to cause trouble
> in your family. If you'd rather not pursue it, I'll leave it there and thank you for reading.

## Before either is sent

- **They go through MyHeritage's own messaging**, not email — that is how the matches are reachable,
  and it keeps their addresses private.
- **Send B first.** She is an experienced genealogist with a documented tree and is the likelier to
  answer usefully; her reply may make letter A unnecessary.
- Decide whether you want your own name on them or Derrick's. The drafts say *"a family member"*,
  which is accurate and leaves the choice open.
- **Derrick should probably be told before a stranger is.** That is not my call, and it is the reason
  neither of these has gone.
