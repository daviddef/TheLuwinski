## What this asks for and why it is the best remaining shot

Row 33 established that the South African **civil marriage register never asked for parents** —
not in 1940, not in 1948, not in 1956. That is why this archive cannot document the Wear sibship
from South African sources, and why everything rests on a seven-person family tree built in 2018.

**A deceased estate does ask.** Form **J294**, the death notice, has a field for the deceased's
**father's full names** and **mother's full names**. So the shortest route to breaking the archive's
dependence on that tree is not Leonard's own estate — he appears to have none — but **his sisters'**.

Three are dead, and all three were born at Hitchin to Martha Maud Castle:

| | born | died | married name | source for the death |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Janette Eileen Maud EVANS**, formerly Bailey | **28 Oct 1919** | **2001** | Evans | Verified death index; and her own naturalisation certificate **R54033** of 30 Oct 1975 gives the birth date |
| **Kathleen Edith FOOT** | **1921** | **2010** | Foot (m. 1945, James Cecil Foot) | Verified death index **and estate-file index** |
| **Cynthia Yvonne Elizabeth HALSEY**, born Wear | **1931** | **2000** | Halsey | Verified death index; her divorce is **WLD 12617/78** |

**If any one J294 names HENRY ROBERT WEAR and MARTHA MAUD CASTLE, this archive has, for the first
time, a South African government record stating that couple as the parents of a child.** It does not
prove Leonard was theirs. It removes the family tree as the only source for the couple, which is the
weakest joint in the whole chain.

## Why they cannot be ordered by number

Checked before drafting, so the letter does not ask the Master to find something the sender could
have found himself:

- **The Government Gazette carries no estate notice for any of the three.** `"CYNTHIA YVONNE
  ELIZABETH"` returns nothing at all; `"JANETTE EILEEN MAUD"` returns only her 1975 naturalisation
  notice and the 1976 certificate list; `"KATHLEEN EDITH" FOOT` returns nothing in 2009–2014.
- **That is expected, not suspicious.** A Form J193 advertisement is required only above a value
  threshold. Three women who died in a Johannesburg suburb in 2000, 2001 and 2010 would very
  probably fall under it.
- **And NAAIRS cannot help**: its deceased-estate index stops at the end of 1976 — 2,246 estate
  records in 1976, one in 1977, zero thereafter.

**So they must be asked for by name and date of death.** That is an ordinary counter request, and it
is what this letter does.

## If the Master's office declines

The **Department of Home Affairs** holds the death register entry, which names the informant but not
the parents, so it is not a substitute. The better fallback is **row 61** — the naturalisation file
behind certificate **R54033**, granted to Janette Eileen Maud Evans on 30 October 1975, which
records date and place of birth, previous nationality **and parents**, and which sits with Home
Affairs or with the National Archives in Pretoria rather than with the Master.
