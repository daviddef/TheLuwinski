Dear Sir or Madam,

An addition to my enquiry of 15 September about WEAR, Leonard Aubrey Robert, at Roberts Heights in 1940.

I asked in that letter whether the medal rolls might yield a force number, and said that with a number the rest becomes ordinary. I think I now have it, from the British side, and it should save you most of the work I asked for.

    Force number      141272
    Rank              Corporal
    Force             South Africa Union Defence Forces
    Status            Prisoner of war, held in Germany
    Listed            1943, 1944 and 1945

The source is British, not South African: The National Archives at Kew, WO 392/15 and WO 392/21 — the War Office Directorate of Prisoners of War lists for 1943 to 1945. The same number appears against him in five separate entries across those two volumes.

SO QUESTIONS 3, 4 AND 5 OF MY EARLIER LETTER FALL AWAY. You do not need to search the medal rolls to establish a number, and you do not need a nominal index. If 141272 is the right man, it is the ordinary key to your holdings.

ONE HONEST CAVEAT, WHICH I WOULD RATHER STATE THAN HAVE YOU DISCOVER

The prisoner-of-war lists carry INITIALS ONLY — "L A R Wear" — and no date of birth. My identification rests on four things: the initials with that surname; service in the Union Defence Force rather than a British regiment; an age that fits, since he was born in 1917 and sailed from Southampton to the Cape in March 1937; and the one number repeating consistently across five records.

That is strong, but it is not proof, and I am conscious that I may be sending you a number that belongs to somebody else. If 141272 turns out to be a different man entirely, that is a useful answer and I would be glad to be told it plainly.

WHAT I AM NOW ASKING

1. The personnel or attestation record for force number 141272, if it survives.

2. Whatever it gives for his PARENTS or NEXT OF KIN. This remains the heart of the enquiry, for the reason I gave before: South African civil marriage registers of this period have no column for parents, and an attestation form does. It is the one thing I cannot presently document.

3. His PRISONER-OF-WAR AND REPATRIATION PAPERS, if those are held separately from the ordinary service file. I mention it only because I understand they sometimes are, and asking for both now may save us a second exchange.

4. THE ADDRESSES GIVEN ON ENLISTMENT AND ON DISCHARGE, if the file records them. He is untraceable in the ordinary sources between 1941 and 1950, which I now understand rather better than I did — a man in a prison camp does not appear in a directory or on a voters' roll — and a discharge address would be the first thing to place him after the war.

Everything else in my earlier letter stands, including the two disclosures: I am not a descendant of this man and make no claim to be, and I am happy to complete any form, pay any fee, supply identification, and wait.

With thanks, and with apologies for adding to a thread rather than waiting quietly.

Yours faithfully,
