# Request 7 — a Stolperstein for Jacob Leibholz, Meraner Straße 2

**There is no stone for him.** Checked 14 September 2026 against the Wikipedia register of the 738
Stolpersteine in Berlin-Schöneberg and the Stolpersteine-Berlin database: **none at Meraner Straße,
and none for any Leibholz in the district.** The Leibholz stones that do exist in Berlin —
Willmanndamm 6, Danckelmannstraße 44 — belong to other Leibholz families.

Until today the address was not known. It is now.

## What is documented, and where

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Jacob [Jakob] Leibholz |
| **Born** | 8 August 1884, Schubin (Szubin), Posen |
| **Occupation** | *Kaufmann* |
| **Last address** | **Meraner Straße 2, Berlin-Schöneberg** |
| **Deported** | **from Berlin, 26 February 1943, to Auschwitz** — a transport of 913–1,095 people, the day before the *Fabrikaktion* |
| **Died** | Not documented. The Bundesarchiv deliberately records no date |

**Sources.** Bundesarchiv, *Gedenkbuch*, entry **en1100957** (birth, residence, deportation).
*Mapping the Lives*, from the census of **17 May 1939** (the address, and the household).
Yad Vashem, Central Database of Shoah Victims' Names (murdered; *List of Deportation from Berlin*;
*List of Murdered Jews from Germany*).

## The household at that address, also documented

- **Auguste Leibholz née Luwinski**, born 28 August 1893 — his wife, not Jewish, registered at the
  same address in the 1939 census. She survived and died at Kraichtal in 1975.
- **Siegfried Leibholz**, born 5 August 1925 at Martin-Luther-Straße 60 — their son, aged 14 in
  1939. He was moved out to Sommerfeld in **January 1943**, weeks before his father was taken, and
  stayed there until 30 April 1945. He survived, joined the Berlin Kreispolizei in 1947, and died at
  Potsdam in 2005.
- And an older son who was not there: **Kurt Israel Luwinski**, born Berlin 11 May 1912, who left
  for Johannesburg in **1933** and never returned. He is absent from every Berlin persecution
  record — the gap is the emigration.

## Who to write to

- **Koordinierungsstelle Stolpersteine Berlin** — the citywide coordinating office, which handles
  applications, research and the laying list.
- **Bezirksamt Tempelhof-Schöneberg**, `Stolpersteine@ba-ts.berlin.de` — the borough Meraner Straße
  falls in, which maintains the local register and runs the laying ceremonies.

>
> Er lebte dort mit seiner nichtjüdischen Ehefrau **Auguste Leibholz, geb. Luwinski** (geb. 28.
> August 1893), und dem gemeinsamen Sohn **Siegfried Leibholz** (geb. 5. August 1925), der überlebte.
> Ein älterer Sohn, **Kurt Luwinski** (geb. 11. Mai 1912 in Berlin), emigrierte 1933 nach

>
> Ich bin ein Nachkomme dieser Familie und würde die Patenschaft für den Stein gerne übernehmen.
> Über eine Rückmeldung zum weiteren Vorgehen würde ich mich sehr freuen.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen,

## One honest note before sending

David's descent from Jacob Leibholz runs through Kurt, and this archive's central finding is that
**Kurt is very probably not Derrick's biological father** — which would make the relationship one of
family rather than blood. That does not disqualify anyone from sponsoring a stone; sponsors are
frequently neighbours, schools and strangers. But the letter says *"ein Nachkomme dieser Familie"*,
a descendant of this family, and if the Y-DNA comes back as expected that wording should change to
something truthful and just as good — *the family my father was born into*.
