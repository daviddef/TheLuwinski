Subject: Thank you — and one further question about the parish's own registers

Dear Father, dear Parish Secretary,

Thank you very much for your reply and for the scan of the marriage register, which reached me on
22 September. I am grateful both for the trouble and for the speed; I know a parish office has more
pressing work than a stranger's family history, and I did not take an answer for granted.

The document you sent is the ORIGINAL MARRIAGE REGISTER, the Government form B.M.D. 3, entry number
13 of 1940 — the register Father W. P. Whelan completed in his capacity as marriage officer for the
State. It is a good scan and I am glad to have it.

MAY I ASK ABOUT TWO OTHER BOOKS

What I am hoping for is the parish's own sacramental record of the same wedding, which is a
different book from the Government form:

    1. The PARISH MARRIAGE REGISTER — the Liber Matrimoniorum, the register the parish keeps for
       itself. A Catholic marriage entry of 1940 ordinarily records THE NAMES OF THE FATHER AND
       MOTHER OF BOTH PARTIES.

    2. The BANNS REGISTER. Leonard Wear and Phyllis Robertson were married by banns rather than on
       a licence, so their intention was read in church on three Sundays beforehand, and that
       generates its own entry.

WHY THE PARENTS' NAMES MATTER SO MUCH

This is the whole of my difficulty. South African civil marriage registers of this period have no
column for the parents of either party — the form you kindly sent has none — and neither do the
civil registers in England for the years I need. Catholic sacramental registers do.

    LEONARD AUBREY ROBERT WEAR, aged 23, born in England, a soldier stationed at Roberts Heights,
    married at your church on 7 SEPTEMBER 1940

My father-in-law is an elderly man in Australia who has never known who his father was. The
evidence points to Leonard Wear, and points hard, but every step of it is circumstantial — DNA
matches, a census, an index. A parish register naming Leonard's own father and mother would be the
first document to state his parentage outright, and there is nowhere else left that I know of to
look for one.

IF THE REGISTERS ARE NOT WITH YOU

I understand that parish books of 1940 are sometimes deposited with the Archdiocese rather than
kept in the parish. If that is the case here, I would be grateful simply to be told where the
1940 registers are held, and I will write to them instead and trouble you no further.

I am glad to pay any fee, to complete any form, to supply identification, and to wait for as long
as it takes. I should say plainly that I am not a descendant of anyone in this record and make no
claim to be: I am researching on behalf of my wife's father.
