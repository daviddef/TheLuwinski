# Request 15 — South African High Commission, Canberra: procedure for vault copies

**Status: DRAFTED 15 September 2026. NOT SENT.** Drafted because David asked for the work list to
be worked, not because the archive has decided to approach anyone. **Do not send without David.**

## Why this is not a letter to Home Affairs

Row 22 of the work list was written as *"ask Home Affairs for what is filed behind marriage entry
858 of 1948."* Before drafting it, the sent mail was searched, per the standing rule. It turned up a
reply the archive had never taken account of — **Heather MacAlister, Ancestors Research South
Africa, 11 May 2020**, to David, about the Lerena side:

> Birth certificates or any kind of certificate such as death or marriage, whether it be abridged,
> unabridged or vault **can only be applied for in person (only) at any Department of Home
> Affairs**. If the person applying for it is not for them then **only immediate family members can
> apply**. Home Affairs no longer allows 3rd party applications so I cannot do it on your behalf and
> any company that offers this service offers an illegal service … If, however you live overseas you
> can then apply through the **South African Embassy or Consulate in your country of residence**.

That is six years old and it is one researcher's account of policy rather than the policy itself.
But it is the best evidence this archive has, and **it says plainly that an emailed research request
to Home Affairs is not a mechanism that exists.** Sending one would have been the fourth dead
address of this project. It was avoided by searching the sent mail, which is exactly what that rule
was written for after the Xaba duplication.

## Two consequences, one of them uncomfortable

**1. The route for an overseas applicant is the High Commission, not the department.** Hence this
draft, addressed to Canberra.

**2. "Only immediate family members can apply" points at one person.** For the 1948 marriage of
Kurt Luwinski and Ivone de Witt, the immediate family are the two spouses, both long dead, and
**their son of record, Derrick.** So if the rule is as stated, the Home Affairs route to that
document runs through Derrick and nobody else.

**That collides directly with work-list row 15** — whether Derrick should be told before any more
strangers are. This archive has raised row 15 twice and been overruled twice, and it is not raising
it a third time here. It is recording that **the two questions have now met**: this particular
document cannot be obtained without him. **David's call, and only David's.**

## What this draft therefore asks

It asks **how**, not **for the record**. A procedural enquiry costs nothing, commits nobody, needs
no identity documents, and cannot be refused for lack of standing. If the answer is that a
descendant by marriage may apply, the substantive request follows. If the answer is that only a
child may, then row 15 has to be settled first, by David.

**Subject:** Procedure for obtaining a vault copy of a 1948 marriage record from an overseas applicant

---

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing from Australia with a **procedural question only**. I am not, at this stage, applying
for anything, and I have not enclosed any documents.

I am compiling a documented family history and I need to know what is possible before I ask for it.

**The record.** A marriage solemnised at the Magistrate's Office, Johannesburg, on **19 June 1948**:

- **Kurt Luwinski**, aged 36, born Berlin, Germany
- **Ivone de Witt, born de Figueiredo**, aged 24, born Portuguese East Africa
- **Duplicate Original Marriage Register, Johannesburg, no. 858 of 1948**
- Married on special licence no. 164531, dated 9 June 1948

I have read the register page itself on microfilm (FamilySearch, digital film 008109682, frame
2047), so I already have the eleven columns. **What I do not have is what the film does not show:**

1. The **reverse** of the sheet. The printed form ends with the instruction *[P.T.O.*, and the
   microfilm holds only the fronts — the frames on either side of it are different certificates.
   Whatever is written on the back of entry 858 survives only in the register itself.
2. Whatever was **filed with the entry**. Column 11 of the front carries the remark
   **"Order Filed."**, which I understand to mean that a court order — the decree dissolving the
   bride's earlier marriage — was produced on the day and filed with the register.

**My questions are these:**

1. May an applicant resident in Australia apply through this High Commission for a **vault copy** of
   a marriage record of 1948, and if so what is the form, the fee, and the expected time?
2. Does a vault copy reproduce **both sides** of the register sheet, or only the face of it?
3. Are documents **filed with** an entry — such as an order noted in the Remarks column — retrievable
   at all, and if so by what request and from which office?
4. **Who is eligible to apply for a record of this age?** I have been told that applications are
   limited to the person named or their immediate family. Both parties to this 1948 marriage died
   decades ago. I would be grateful to know whether, for a record of this age, eligibility extends to
   descendants, and what proof of relationship is required.

If any of this is properly a question for the Department of Home Affairs directly, or for the
National Archives rather than the department, I would be grateful simply to be pointed there.

I am happy to pay the ordinary fees and to be patient. My thanks for your time.

Yours faithfully,
