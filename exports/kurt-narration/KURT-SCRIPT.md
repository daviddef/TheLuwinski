# Kurt Luwinski, in his own voice
### A narration script. Every fact is documented; every silence is marked.
*Runs about four and a half minutes read slowly. Source for each passage in the margin notes.*

---

**[1934 photograph — the young man, smiling]**

My name is Kurt Luwinski. I was born in Berlin on the eleventh of May, nineteen twelve.

You can look me up. Standesamt Berlin the Eighth, birth register for that year, number
nine hundred and sixty-three. They wrote me down as **Kurt Myszislaus**. Nobody ever called me
Myszislaus.

My mother was Auguste. Lizzy, to the family. She was a milliner — a *Modistin* — and she was
born out east, at Sauerbaum in the Kreis Rössel, in eighteen ninety-three. My father was Jacob
Leibholz, a merchant, born at Schubin.

They married in January nineteen twenty-four, at the Schöneberg registry office. **I was eleven
years old.** My brother Siegfried came the year after.

> *Birth: Standesamt Berlin VIII, Geburtsregister 1912 Nr. 963. Parents' marriage: Standesamt
> Schöneberg II, Heiratsregister 1924 Nr. 6, read at the image. Siegfried b. 5 Aug 1925.*

---

**[Hold on the 1934 photograph]**

The family says I left for South Africa in nineteen thirty-three.

**The family is wrong, or not quite right.** On the thirtieth of January, nineteen thirty-four,
I took out a driving licence in Berlin. Class three. Issued by the Police President. Four
Reichsmarks of Prussian stamps on it. My address on the paper is **Meraner Straße 2, in
Schöneberg** — my parents' flat.

A man who had gone already does not do that.

So either I went later, or I went and came back. **I cannot tell you which. Nobody wrote it
down,** and the paper only says where I was that day.

> *Führerschein Klasse 3, Polizeipräsident Berlin, 30 January 1934, read at the image. The 1933
> emigration year comes from the family tree and no document. The contradiction is published as a
> withdrawn fact, not resolved.*

---

**[Transition toward the 1939 portrait]**

Weeks later I took out an international permit, for driving outside Germany.

Its identity page — name, forename, place of birth, date of birth, where I lived — **is blank.
Five empty lines, struck through with one loop of a pen.** The booklet was issued, stamped and
photographed, and nobody ever filled it in.

I have no explanation for that either.

> *Internationaler Führerschein, Berlin 1934. Page 3, "Angaben über den Führer": photograph and
> Polizeipräsident stamp present, all five fields empty and struck through.*

---

**[1939 portrait — hold here for the rest]**

The twenty-sixth of July, nineteen thirty-nine. The German Consulate in Johannesburg.

They gave me a passport. Under *Beruf*, trade, they wrote **Hutmacher** — hatter. Under
*Wohnort*, where I lived, they stamped **Johannesburg**. Medium build, oval face, brown eyes,
brown hair, no distinguishing marks. Where it said wife, and where it said children, they drew a
line through.

And at the bottom, where the holder signs, I wrote my name.

**Israel Kurt Luwinski.**

I did not choose that middle name. The decree of nineteen thirty-eight gave it to every Jewish
man in Germany, and a consulate four thousand miles from Berlin, with an eagle and a swastika on
its stamp, applied it to me and had me sign under it.

**Why they counted me as a Jew and not as a half-Jew, I do not know.** That question is still
open. What I can tell you is the date, and that the hand is mine.

The passport was good for one year. Until the twenty-fifth of July, nineteen forty.

> *Reisepass, Deutsches Konsulat Johannesburg, 26 July 1939, read at the image; signature,
> Personenbeschreibung and validity all transcribed. Why the consulate classified him as a Jew
> rather than a Mischling is recorded as an open question.*

---

My mother made hats. Her brother-in-law sold them wholesale — *Hüte en gros*. I was a hatter.

On the first of November, nineteen forty-one, I bought the **Piccadilly Hat Company, at
eighty-one Eloff Street**, Johannesburg, with a man named Walter Oppenheimer. A year later to the
day I took it over alone. I was still trading there in nineteen fifty-two.

So when people say I fell into the hat trade in Africa — no. **I was a hatter before I had a
shop.** It came down my mother's side.

> *Union Government Gazette partnership notices, 30 Oct 1942 and after; Gazettes.Africa. The
> 1939 passport gives "Hutmacher" two years before the purchase.*

---

**[Slow here]**

While I was selling hats in Eloff Street, my parents divorced in Berlin. The twenty-first of
March, nineteen forty.

And on the twenty-sixth of February, nineteen forty-three, my father Jacob was put on the
thirtieth East transport, from a room at Treuchtlinger Straße 4, and taken to Auschwitz.

**I do not know when I learned of it. I do not know whether I knew at all.** There is no letter,
no cable, nothing in any file this family has found. The record ends where he does.

> *Divorce: Landgericht Berlin 251. R. 80. 40, final 21 March 1940, noted in the margin of the
> 1924 marriage entry. Deportation: Bundesarchiv Gedenkbuch and the 30th Osttransport list.
> Whether Kurt knew, and when, is not documented and is not asserted.*

---

**[Lift slightly]**

I married Ivone on the nineteenth of June, nineteen forty-eight, at Johannesburg. I was
thirty-six. The register has me down as a bachelor.

It did not last. I filed that same year, and the decree came in nineteen fifty-one. I married
again in fifty-six and filed again in fifty-seven, and that year the bank sued me too.

**Those are the papers. They are not the whole of it, and I would not pretend they are.** A court
file records that a marriage ended. It does not record a single thing about why, or what was good
in it before.

> *Marriage: Johannesburg, 19 June 1948, FamilySearch civil marriage record and the register image.
> Divorces: WLD 1950/1948, WLD 497/1951, WLD 2368/1951. Second marriage 29 Feb 1956, entry No. 169,
> status "divorcee". 1957 action and the bank's suit from NAAIRS and the gazettes.*

---

**[Quiet]**

There is a boy in this story, and he is the reason you are listening.

**Derrick.** I raised him.

A test done long after I was gone says the blood is not mine. I never knew that, and I will not
tell you what I would have made of it. **What the record says is that I raised him** — and that
is the part I would want kept.

> *Paternity: autosomal DNA, 0.0% Ashkenazi where a quarter is required, and the Y-line result.
> "Kurt raised him" is Derrick's own testimony, 2026. Kurt's knowledge or view is not documented
> and is not invented here.*

---

**[Final]**

I died at Johannesburg on the fifteenth of December, nineteen sixty-eight. Fifty-six years old.
They buried me the next day at Westpark — block L, grave two hundred and eleven.

For a long time my family had the year wrong. They had me dying in sixty-nine. It took somebody
reading a burial register to put me back in the right year.

**That is most of what I am now: a date in a register, read carefully by somebody who cared
whether it was right.**

There are worse things to be.

> *Chevrah Kadisha burial register: died 15.12.1968, buried 16.12.1968, Westpark block L grave 211,
> Yahrzeit 24 Kislev 5729. The archive's own correction of the 1969 death year is published.*

---

## What this script will not say, and why

These were left out deliberately. **If you add them, the film stops being true.**

- **Why he left Germany.** No document gives a reason. Not persecution, not opportunity, not
  family — nothing.
- **Whether he knew his father had been deported.** Nothing survives either way.
- **What he felt about Derrick's paternity.** There is no evidence he ever knew.
- **His religion, in his own mind.** The state called him a Jew in 1938 and a consulate wrote
  "Israel" on his passport. The archive does not know what he called himself.
- **A sweetheart, a war, a politics.** None of it is in the record. Inventing any of it would be
  the easiest thing in the world and it would ruin this.
