# Virtual Kurt — the prompt

## The image to use

**`kurt-1939-portrait-enhanced.jpg`** — the primary. This is **Kurt's own passport photograph**,
taken for the German passport issued to him at the German Consulate in Johannesburg on
**26 July 1939**. He is twenty-seven. It is a real photograph of the real man, dated to the day,
and on the same page he signed his name in his own hand.

Use it as the likeness source for an avatar tool (HeyGen, Synthesia, D-ID, Runway Act-One, etc.).
It is three-quarter face, evenly lit, eyes clear and open, and large enough after upscaling.

**`kurt-1934-portrait-enhanced.jpg`** — the alternate, for the opening. Kurt at **twenty-one**,
from the photograph in his 1934 international driving permit: in profile, **smiling**. Use this if
you want the film to begin with the young man in Berlin and age into the 1939 face.

## Likeness notes, taken from his own passport — not invented

The 1939 **Personenbeschreibung** describes him officially:

| | |
|---|---|
| Build (*Gestalt*) | **mittel** — medium |
| Face (*Gesicht*) | **oval** |
| Eyes (*Farbe der Augen*) | **braun** — brown |
| Hair (*Farbe des Haares*) | **braun** — brown |
| Distinguishing marks | **none** |
| Trade (*Beruf*) | **Hutmacher** — hatter |

If a tool asks you to describe him, use those words. They are the state's own description of him,
written on the day.

## The avatar prompt

> A man of twenty-seven, photographed in Johannesburg in July 1939. Medium build, oval face,
> brown eyes, brown hair worn swept back from a high forehead, clean-shaven, no distinguishing
> marks. He wears a tweed jacket, white shirt and tie. He speaks quietly and directly to camera,
> in English with a light Berlin-German accent — the accent of a man who grew up in Schöneberg and
> has been in South Africa a few years. **He is not performing.** He is a tradesman, a hatter, not
> an orator: his delivery is plain, unhurried, a little dry. He pauses before the hard parts and
> does not dramatise them. He does not weep. Warm, measured, with the faint humour of someone who
> has told this before and still finds parts of it strange.

## Three rules for whoever edits this

**1. Every fact in the script is documented.** Dates, places, register numbers and trades all come
from records this archive has read. Nothing in the narration is invented biography. If you add a
line, add a source with it.

**2. Where the record is silent, he says so.** The script has Kurt admitting what is not known —
why he left, what he felt, when he learned of his father. **Do not replace those with guesses.**
They are the most honest moments in it and they are what keeps the film a memorial rather than a
fiction.

**3. Label it.** On screen at the start or end: *a reconstruction, built from documents — the voice
is not a recording.* His photograph is real; his words are not his own. Say so plainly, once.
